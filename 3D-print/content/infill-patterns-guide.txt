COMPREHENSIVE GUIDE: Infill Patterns & Print Optimization for 3D Printed Magnet Frames

WHAT IS INFILL:
Infill is the internal structure of a 3D print. Instead of printing solid objects (which wastes plastic and time), 3D printers create an internal lattice pattern. For magnet photo frames, we don't need extreme strength — they simply hang on a fridge — so infill optimization is about saving time and money.

INFILL PERCENTAGE GUIDE:
- 0% (completely hollow): Too fragile. The frame will crush under light pressure. Never use this.
- 10%: Minimum viable for magnet frames. Very lightweight. Works for small wallet-size frames under 65x90mm. Saves the most material and print time.
- 15%: Our recommended default for most production frames. Excellent balance of strength, weight, speed, and material cost. This is what 90% of your frames should use.
- 20%: Use for larger frames (4x6 and above) or frames with thin borders under 10mm. Adds minimal time but noticeably more rigidity.
- 100% (completely solid): Never use for entire frames. Wastes 3-4x the material and takes 2-3x longer. However, use 100% infill selectively around magnet slot areas for maximum magnet hold strength.

INFILL PATTERNS RANKED FOR MAGNET FRAME PRODUCTION:
1. GRID PATTERN (★★★★★ — Our Default):
   - Structure: perpendicular lines creating a grid of squares
   - Speed: Very fast to print (minimal direction changes)
   - Strength: Good in both X and Y directions (bidirectional)
   - Why for frames: Fast print + adequate strength = maximum profit per frame
   - Use this for: All standard production frames

2. GYROID PATTERN (★★★★☆ — Premium Choice):
   - Structure: Continuous 3D wave pattern (no straight lines)
   - Speed: 10-15% slower than grid
   - Strength: Strongest infill per gram of material used
   - Why for frames: Use for premium/heavy frames like multi-photo collages
   - Also provides some flexibility (frames bounce instead of crack if dropped)
   - Visual appeal: If you cut a frame in half for display, gyroid looks incredible

3. LINES PATTERN (★★★☆☆):
   - Structure: Parallel lines alternating direction each layer
   - Speed: Fastest infill pattern available
   - Strength: Strong in one direction only (weak perpendicular)
   - Use for: Quick draft prints, testing, small single-direction frames

4. HONEYCOMB PATTERN (★★☆☆☆):
   - Structure: Hexagonal cells (like a beehive)
   - Speed: Significantly slower (many direction changes)
   - Strength: Very high but overkill for frames
   - Skip this: No advantage over gyroid for frames, but much slower

5. TRI-HEXAGONAL (★★☆☆☆):
   - Beautiful internal geometry but no practical advantage for frames
   - Print time penalty not justified

WALL COUNT (Shell Thickness):
- 2 walls (0.8mm total): Absolute minimum. Frame edges may appear slightly translucent with light colors.
- 3 walls (1.2mm total): Our default recommendation. Solid feel, great surface for sanding and painting. No light bleed-through.
- 4 walls (1.6mm total): Premium feel. Adds about 10% print time. Worth it for retail frames above $12 price point. The frame feels substantial in hand — customers notice quality.

TOP AND BOTTOM SOLID LAYERS:
- 3 layers minimum (at 0.2mm layer height = 0.6mm total solid top/bottom)
- 4 layers recommended (0.8mm — fully opaque, smooth, no infill patterns showing through)
- The top surface of a frame is what customers see first. Under-layering causes visible infill texture.

VARIABLE INFILL (Advanced — PrusaSlicer Feature):
This is a game-changer for magnet frames. PrusaSlicer allows "modifier meshes" — invisible zones where you change infill settings:
- Place a 100% infill modifier around each magnet slot location
- Keep the rest of the frame at 15% infill
- Result: Magnets are held in rock-solid plastic, while the frame itself is light and fast to print
- This saves 40-50% material compared to printing the entire frame at high infill
- Cura equivalent: Use "support blocker" mesh with per-model settings changed to infill override

PRINT SPEED VS QUALITY OPTIMIZATION:
- 40mm/s: Maximum quality. Use for display/portfolio pieces only.
- 50mm/s: Safe default. All printers handle this well.
- 60-80mm/s: Production speed. Slight quality reduction, most printers handle it.
- 100-150mm/s: High-speed printers only (Bambu Lab, Prusa MK4 with Input Shaping). Quality remains good due to vibration compensation.

RETRACTION SETTINGS FOR FRAME PRODUCTION:
- Bowden extruder: 5-6mm distance, 45mm/s speed
- Direct drive extruder: 1-2mm distance, 35mm/s speed
- Retraction helps eliminate stringing between frame features (magnet slots, text, clip details)

BATCH PRINTING OPTIMIZATION:
- Arrange multiple frames on one build plate: 9 wallet frames or 4 standard 4x6 frames on a 220mm bed
- Sequential printing (one-at-a-time): Safer — if one fails, others already completed
- Simultaneous printing (all-at-once): Faster total time but one failure ruins entire batch
- Our recommendation: Sequential for expensive filament (PETG, specialty), simultaneous for PLA production runs
- Per-unit effective time drops from 45 minutes to 28 minutes in batch mode — a 38% time savings

LAYER HEIGHT COMPARISON FOR FRAMES:
- 0.12mm: Premium quality, almost invisible layer lines. Takes 2.5 hours per 4x6 frame. Use for portfolio pieces only.
- 0.16mm: High quality, faint layer lines. Takes 1.8 hours. Good for retail over $15.
- 0.20mm: Our production default. Takes 1.2 hours. Excellent quality-to-speed ratio. Layer lines disappear after light sanding + primer.
- 0.28mm: Draft quality, visible layer lines. Takes 50 minutes. Use for prototyping and testing designs.
