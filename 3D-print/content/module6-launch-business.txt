COST PER UNIT BREAKDOWN
The real numbers behind every magnet frame you produce: Filament cost for a standard frame uses about 30g of PLA+ at $20/kg equals $0.60. Electricity per print averages $0.03. Two neodymium magnets at $0.15 each equals $0.30. Primer and paint per frame equals $0.10. Packaging materials equals $0.15. Wear and tear estimate equals $0.10. Shipping supplies equals $0.35. Total material cost per frame: approximately $1.63. At a $12 retail price, that's an 86% gross margin before labor and overhead. Even at $8 wholesale to boutiques, you maintain 80% margins.

PRICING STRATEGY ($5-$15 RETAIL)
The pricing sweet spot for magnet frames: Basic single frames $5-8 at craft fairs (impulse buy range). Standard frames $8-12 online (competitive with mass-produced frames). Premium or custom frames $12-15 (personalized, multi-photo, specialty designs). Themed sets of 3-5 frames $20-35 (holiday sets, baby milestone sets, pet lover sets). Bundle deals: "Buy 3 get 1 free" encourages higher cart value. Price anchoring: always display your premium frames first, making standard frames feel affordable by comparison.

SHOPIFY STORE SETUP — FULL WALKTHROUGH
Setting up your Shopify store: Choose a clean theme (Dawn is free and professional). Create product listings with 4-5 high-quality photos per frame — front view, angle view, on-fridge lifestyle shot, detail close-up, size reference. Write descriptions that sell the experience, not just the product. Set up Shopify Payments (no transaction fees). Create collections: "Best Sellers", "New Arrivals", "Baby Milestones", "Pet Lovers". Add shipping rates or offer free shipping over twenty-five dollars. Install reviews app for social proof.

CRAFT FAIR STRATEGY
Finding events: search "craft fairs near me", check local Facebook groups, join craft fair vendor Facebook communities, look at Eventbrite. Application timeline: apply 2-3 months ahead for popular fairs. Booth setup: use a small table with a tablecloth, vertical display board to show frames at eye level, magnetic display surface so customers can interact. Price cards should be clear and visible. Accept card payments with Square or Stripe reader. Bring business cards linking to your online store. Target 8-12 fairs per year, rotating between locations.

PRODUCT PHOTOGRAPHY
Great photos sell products. Use natural light near a large window. Set frames on a clean white or light wood background. Shoot at slight angles to show depth. Include lifestyle shots: frame on a fridge with a real photo inside, frame on a desk, frame being held for scale. Use a smartphone with portrait mode for professional-looking depth of field. Edit in Snapseed or Lightroom Mobile: increase brightness, adjust white balance, enhance shadows. Maintain consistent style across all product photos for brand cohesion.

PACKAGING & SHIPPING
Packaging that impresses: Place frame in a clear poly bag. Add a branded sticker or kraft paper belly band. Include a small thank-you card with care instructions and a QR code linking to your store. For shipping: use a rigid mailer or small box padded with recycled paper crinkle. Frame dimensions typically fit USPS First Class Package rates at around $4-5 cost. Print labels through Pirate Ship for discounted USPS rates. Always add fragile stickers for frames with installed magnets.

SCALING: HOBBY TO FULL-TIME INCOME
Phase 1 — Side Hustle: One printer, 4-8 frames per day, one craft fair per month, target $200-500 per month. Phase 2 — Serious Side Income: Two printers running overnight batch prints, online store launched, 3-4 fairs per month, target $1000-2000 per month. Phase 3 — Full-Time: Three to four printers, hiring first helper for post-processing, wholesale accounts with 2-3 local boutiques, Etsy plus Shopify stores, target $3000-5000 per month. Phase 4 — Business: Five plus printers, dedicated workspace, part-time employee, Amazon Handmade listing, content marketing via social media, target $5000-10000 per month. Key scaling lever: batch production assembly line (print → sand → prime → paint → magnets → QC → package).
